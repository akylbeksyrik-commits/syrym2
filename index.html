01 — Introduction

Joining the Secretariat

I want to become a member of the Secretariat because I already know this conference from the inside — as a Helper, as a Delegate, and as someone who organizes events for people.

A Secretariat is not a decoration. It is the team that holds the conference together: time, people, information, atmosphere, and the small decisions that decide whether a day feels professional or chaotic. I am applying because I have already stood on both sides of that work.

From 2023 to 2025 I was a Helper. In 2026 I became a Delegate. I am not applying because the role sounds impressive. I am applying because I want to contribute, to use what I already know, to grow as a leader, and to help create a successful conference — one that participants trust.

02 — Experience in Model United Nations

From Helper to Delegate

Four conferences. Two roles. One conclusion: a great MUN is built by people who care about the participants as much as they care about the debate.

Role I · 2023–2025

MUN Helper

For three years I worked behind the scenes. A Helper is the person participants meet when they need a direction, a paper, a reminder, or a calm answer. I learned how to stay useful when the room is loud, how to support chairs and delegates, and how to keep the conference moving without making myself the center of it.

Helper work taught me the real structure of MUN: registration, committees, timing, materials, communication, and the invisible details that make people feel welcome. I saw that organization is not only a plan on paper. It is attention, again and again.

Role II · 2026

MUN Delegate

In 2026 I stepped onto the floor as a Delegate. I spoke, listened, negotiated, and followed procedure from the other side of the table. That year changed my view of the Secretariat and of Helpers: I finally felt what participants need in the moment — clarity, respect, and a conference that does not collapse around them.

Being a Delegate did not erase my Helper experience. It completed it. Now I understand both the organization and the debate. That is why I believe I can serve the Secretariat with more empathy and more precision.

  1. 2023

    Helper — first conference behind the scenes

    My first MUN was not on the speaker’s list. It was in the corridors, in the committee rooms, and in the small tasks that keep a conference alive. I learned names, routes, rules of the day, and the most important skill of all: do not panic when something changes. Watch, help, and stay responsible.

    That year I understood that MUN is a living system. Speeches are only the visible part. The rest is teamwork.

  2. 2024

    Helper — supporting people, not only tasks

    The second year was about people. I already knew the basic structure, so I could look at participants more carefully: who is confused, who needs materials, who is late, who is waiting for an answer. Communication became a real skill — short, polite, and useful.

    I also learned to work inside a team of Helpers. One person cannot hold a conference. A good Helper shares information, follows instructions, and still thinks independently when a problem appears.

  3. 2025

    Helper — responsibility at a higher level

    By the third year I was no longer only learning. I was expected to understand the organization of MUN: how sessions connect, why timing matters, how chairs and Secretariat rely on Helpers, and how one mistake can travel across a whole committee.

    2025 made responsibility personal. I knew the conference well enough to anticipate problems, not only react to them. That is the mindset I want to bring to the Secretariat.

  4. 2026

    Delegate — seeing the conference from the floor

    In 2026 I became a Delegate. I prepared, represented a position, spoke in committee, and worked with other delegates. For the first time I felt the pressure of debate and the value of a conference that is organized with care.

    After three years as a Helper, this role gave me the missing half of the picture. I now know what it feels like to depend on the team that runs the event. I want to be that team.

03 — What MUN taught me

Lessons that stay after the gavel

Conferences end. The skills do not. These five lessons are the reason I feel ready for Secretariat work.

Teamwork

A conference is never one person. It is chairs, Helpers, Secretariat, delegates, and guests moving through the same day. I learned to share work, listen to the team, and put the event above my own comfort.

Communication

In MUN, unclear words create chaos. I learned to speak simply, to confirm information, to stay polite under pressure, and to listen first. Good communication is not loud. It is exact.

Responsibility

People trust you with time, details, and the experience of every participant. If I say I will do something, I do it. If a problem appears, I do not hide it. Responsibility is the core of Secretariat work.

Understanding of MUN organization

After four conferences I understand the map of a MUN: opening, committees, lobbying, sessions, breaks, closing. I know that the beautiful parts of the event survive only if the structure underneath is strong.

Working with participants

Helpers stand next to people. Delegates are people under pressure. I learned to stay calm, respectful, and useful — to answer questions without making anyone feel small. The Secretariat is the face of the conference. I want that face to be professional and human at the same time.

04 — Organization experience

Leadership beyond the committee

MUN is not my only school of organization. Student Council and the STEAM Festival taught me how to plan events, lead a team, and take responsibility for a public result.

Student Council

From member to Vice Leader of Events

  • Member — 2024. I joined the Student Council and learned how student leadership works: meetings, ideas, teamwork, and follow-through.
  • Member — 2025. A second year meant more responsibility. I was no longer new. I had to contribute, not only attend.
  • Vice Leader of Events Committee — 2025–2026. This role is about planning, coordinating people, solving problems before the event, and staying present during it.

Student Council showed me that leadership is service. You do not wait for someone else to make the day work. You prepare, you communicate, and you stay until the work is done.

Other activities

STEAM Festival — 2026

The STEAM Festival is a large public event: science, creativity, people, energy, and a lot of moving parts. Organizing it means thinking about guests, teams, timing, space, and the feeling of the day.

This experience connects directly to Secretariat work. A festival, like a MUN conference, succeeds when participants feel guided and inspired — not lost. I know what it takes to hold an event that is bigger than one person.
